Fixes from a check of every dialog, menu and panel of the widget in a browser. A new name on the layouts button (a layout saved, renamed or opened) refits the toolbar: a longer name pushed "more" past the widget's edge, and pressing it slid the whole widget aside until the window was resized. The widget's box also clips what is past its edges (`overflow: clip`, `hidden` where a browser lacks it), so the keyboard landing past an edge never scrolls it. The watchlist scrolls in the dock, by wheel, touch and keyboard, where 34 symbols in a 768 px window stopped at the panel's foot, and a row the arrow keys reach comes in below the panel's header. Every group of the shortcut sheet scrolls into view: the groups past its height were laid in columns to the side, out of reach, on a phone too. A menu taller than the window (the clock's time zones, 600 px high) fits inside it with its padding and border, where it ran past the bottom by them.

Right to left, the "more" menu, the Trade and Layouts menus and your menus from `addToolbarDropdown` line up under their buttons as the toolbar's own menus do: "more" opened beside its button, toward the toolbar's middle, and the others hung off their buttons' left edge. Left to right, the Trade and Layouts menus, with no room after their start (picked from "more", say), end where their button ends rather than to its left. The right-click and "more" menus are frosted like the other menus when `ui.blur` is above 0 (the Capsule look). The timeframe menu no longer scrolls 3 px either way, the drawing tools no longer scroll by 2 px in a 768 px window in the Print look, and where a narrow chart cuts an indicator's values, those before the cut stay whole and the one it falls in ends in an ellipsis.

`lerpColor` mixes `rgb()` and `rgba()` colours as well as hex, and an alpha as `color-mix` does, so a fade toward a see-through colour keeps the other's hue; a colour it can't read comes back as the end it is nearer, never `rgb(NaN,…)`. `PerformanceDashboard`'s monthly returns fade to its panel's see-through surface at zero, so the months between -5% and +5% took the colour of a month before them (green, the falls too, in a rising year): the falls are red now, fainter toward zero. A `HeatmapChart` labels each cell in white or in dark ink, whichever reads better on it as drawn, so every label reads at 4.5:1 where either ink can: white stayed on cells down to 3:1 (a light theme's month at -3.4% read at 3.1:1, and one near zero barely at all). The dark theme's brightest reds and greens take the dark ink too.
