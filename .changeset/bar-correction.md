**A bar already on the chart can be corrected.** `chart.updateBar(bar)` replaces the loaded bar with the same `time`, wherever it is in the series, and returns `true`. The indicators are computed again from that bar on, Heikin-Ashi, Renko and the other types built from the bars follow, and the view stays where it is, so a feed that learns a closed bar was off no longer needs `setData`, which scrolled to the newest bar, ended a replay and computed everything again. Several corrections before the next frame are computed once, from the earliest. It returns `false`, and changes nothing, when no loaded bar has that time (a new bar is still `appendBar`'s, older ones `prependBars`') or the chart can't take the bar. A correction is no live price: orders and alerts don't follow it and no alert fires on it; alerts on indicator lines aren't checked against the corrected values. A corrected last bar moves the price line, measured from the bar before it, and is where the next price's alert crossing counts from, as after `setData`. During a replay it corrects the live series, shown when the replay ends; a bar in the extended hours while they are hidden shows when they are shown. The bar loaded sent again computes nothing, also without volume or with a high under its close, so a feed that sends its last closed bars on every poll costs nothing for those that didn't change; a missing volume is 0, as with `setData`. The shown array is changed in place, as `updateLastBar` does, so an array you kept from `getData()` sees the new bar. With the WebGL renderer the corrected candle is drawn too.

`dataUpdate` has a new kind, `{ kind: 'correction', time, index }`, sent after the indicators have the corrected bar (none for a hidden bar, during a replay, or for a bar sent again unchanged). In TypeScript, an exhaustive `switch` over `DataUpdatePayload` needs a `'correction'` case. The legend, the crosshair tooltip, the pinned tooltip, the widget's data window and your `crosshairMove` listeners show the corrected values while the pointer rests on that bar or the one after it. In ChartWidget, `widget.getChart().updateBar(bar)` corrects a connected feed's bars; bars given to the widget with `setData` are a copy it builds its timeframes from, so a correction to them lasts until the next timeframe switch (a widget method for these is to come).

**A connected feed corrects a bar by sending it again, closed.** A closed bar (`{ bar, closed: true }`) older than the newest the stream has seen is now the feed's last word on that bar, a venue's final that came after the next bar's first frame or a revision, and goes in as `updateBar`; until now it was dropped. A feed that re-sends outdated closed bars would now apply them. A forming frame older than the newest is still left out, and no longer moves the price line, the orders' P&L or the alerts: a stale frame closing at 90 fired an alert at 95 while every bar stayed at 100. The built-in adapters made a tick from every bar frame's close, a late final's and a stale frame's too, so those moved the price as a live price would: `BinanceAdapter` and `WebSocketAdapter` (`BybitAdapter`, `KrakenAdapter` and your adapters built on it) now make one only from a frame of the newest bar, of those they have sent on the connection and the last of the history they served for its symbol and timeframe (a feed that sends its last closed bar again on subscribing made a tick from it after every reconnect), and `PollingAdapter` (`CoinbaseAdapter`) leaves out an answer older than one it already sent, a slow request overtaken by the next. A `DataAdapter` of your own that makes ticks from its bars should leave older bars out too. On a timeframe built from a finer one, a corrected base bar builds its bar again for the last two built bars before the forming one (`KEPT_BUCKETS`); a correction further back, or to a bar whose base bars were never seen, is left out.

With `aggregateTicks`, the history's last bar is now the one the trades build on: the first trade after connecting kept only itself, so the forming bar lost its open, high, low and volume. A reconnect (the page coming back, a retry, a heartbeat timeout) whose history ends on the bar before the forming one keeps the forming bar's trades; one whose history holds the forming bar takes the history's, which has the trades missed while away. Such a reconnect also set the price line, the orders' P&L and the alerts back to the history's last close, and the chart left out the forming bar until the next trade: with trades at 96 and the history ending at 100, an alert at 98 fired, and the next trade's crossing back over 98 didn't. The forming bar now stays on the chart and the price on its close, for a feed's own bars without `aggregateTicks` too. Its price tag keeps its colour: it showed a fall as a rise until the next trade, as it did whenever a feed sent a frame at an unchanged price (`CurrentPriceLine.setPrice` given no close to measure from keeps the one it has when the price is the same). A trade for a bar already past, delivered late, is left out, where it closed the forming bar early and started it again, and a closed bar for the bar before the forming one corrects it, the forming bar keeping its trades.

`TickAggregator` and `StreamManager`, exported from `@tradecanvas/core` and `@tradecanvas/chart`, change with this. `TickAggregator` keeps the newest bar time it has seen: `processBar` sends a closed bar older than it as the new `correction` event (not `barClose`) and leaves out a forming one, and `processTick` leaves out a trade older than it. `seed(time)` starts it from a history's last bar and doesn't move the newest back, unless the trades built the newer bar on buckets they no longer start on (the exchange's zone came after them, or a reconnect's history starts its 4h bars at the zone's midnight where the first connect had none), a bar it then lets go so that the next trades aren't left out as late until the next bucket; `resume(bar)` makes the history's last bar, still forming, the one the next trades build on unless it is older than the newest, and `reset()` and `setTimeframe()` forget the newest. `StreamManager` seeds it on every connect and switch, puts the bar it was building on top of a reconnect's `snapshot` whose history ends before that bar, with no `priceChange` for the history's last close, sends a correction as the new `barCorrection` event with no `priceChange`, and moves the previous close a price's change is measured from when the corrected bar is the last closed one. It sends `priceChange` at an unchanged price too when that previous close moved, as when a new bar opens at the last close, which is level with it. `GpuFrame` has an optional `seriesRevision`, which moves when a bar of the same array was replaced in place; a GPU renderer of your own that keeps the bars should write them all again then. The Anchored VWAP drawing kept its line by the array, its length and the last bar, so it drew the old line after a correction further back; it now follows the corrected bar. `DrawingPlugin.setDataGetter(getter, revision?)` hands a drawing tool of your own the same revision beside the bars, for what it keeps computed from them.

The React, Vue and Svelte components take a change to one of the last three bars before the forming one (`CORRECTABLE_BARS`), its time the same, as a correction (`updateBar`), not a new series: the view stays. In Vue and Svelte that holds for a bar changed in place too, which the components didn't see before. A change further back is still a new series. `BarFeedHost` has an optional `updateBar`; without it, or when the chart refuses a correction, the bars are given as a new series, once.
