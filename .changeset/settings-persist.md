**The Chart Settings can be read, set and kept.** The settings dialog's switches and choices (the volume and market profiles, swing markers, session shading, prior-period levels, grid, legend, crosshair, scales, time zone, number format) lived only in the widget's memory: a reload, a named layout or a shared link brought them all back to the defaults, and the time zone back to the browser's, and there was no way to read them. `widget.getSettings()` now gives them (a copy, read back from the chart, so what you set on `getChart()` is in it), `widget.setSettings(patch)` changes some, leaving out what it can't read, keeping numbers in the dialog's ranges and recording no undo step, and `widget.onSettingsChange(listener)` is called on each change, once when the dialog closes with OK and never for Cancel or a change that changes nothing; it returns the function that stops it. `ChartSettingsState`, their type, is exported, with a note on every field. With the new `persistSettings: true` (or `{ key }`) the widget keeps what the user changed from your values in this browser and puts it back on the next visit: a kept value wins over `chartOptions` and the features' defaults, Reset goes back to yours, and the log and auto-scale flags and a chart type's own settings are not kept. It is off by default, and each chart of a `ChartWidgetGrid` gets a key of its own. A named layout saved from now on keeps the Chart Settings and opens with them, but not the time zone or the number format, which stay the viewer's (a layout saved before has none and leaves the settings as they are; an older version drops them). A shared link carries the ones changed from the defaults (the legend, volume, bar countdown and value-label switches from where your `features` put them), a flipped scale and a chart type's own settings among them, again without the time zone and the number format, and its scale is now the one a layout opened from it keeps. Stored, shared and layout settings are all checked as they are read (a fixed time zone offset only between UTC-14 and UTC+14), and where your `features` turn off the legend, the volume, the bar countdown or the log scale, none of them turns it on. With `persistSettings` on, what `setSettings` sets and what an opened link or layout brings are kept as the user's too. A listener that throws is reported in the console; the others still hear it, and the dialog's undo step is kept. The widget also starts its settings from `chartOptions.crosshair.mode` and `chartOptions.logScale`, as the chart does.

A link, a layout or a size typed in the dialog can no longer stall the chart. A Renko box far too small for the prices (0.000000001 near 60,000) made the tab lay bricks until it ran out of memory. Renko's box, Kagi's reversal and a Point & Figure box far too small for the prices are now raised as the range of range bars is: to 2, 5, 10, 20… times themselves, the first that makes no more, when the bars before the last would make more than 100,000 bricks, lines or columns, or the last bar that many on its own. Kagi and Point & Figure make one line or column a bar at most, so only a series of more than 100,000 bars can reach that; Line Break makes one line a bar at most too. `toRenko`, `toKagi` and `toPointAndFigure` do the same, and `pointAndFigureColumns` (from `@tradecanvas/core`) gives the columns with the box they were built on, to draw them that size. A size that is no finite number above 0 is still dropped wherever chart type settings are read, the settings dialog takes no box, reversal or range under 0.00000001 and marks a number it turns away in red, and Renko passes over a close that is no finite number, where an infinite one laid bricks without end.
