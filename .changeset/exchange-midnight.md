**Bars the library builds start at the exchange's midnight, not UTC's.** This changes where some bars start. When the chart knows the symbol's zone (for the timeframes a feed lacks, only `SymbolInfo.timeZone` from your adapter's `resolveSymbol`; for bars given to `ChartWidget.setData`, the symbol's zone, else the market's from `setMarket`), the days, weeks, months and the hour frames that divide a day (2h, 3h, 4h, 6h, 8h, 12h) it builds from shorter bars start at that zone's midnight. They used to start at UTC's, so a Ho Chi Minh City 1D bar opened at 07:00 there and a 4H bar split the morning session at 11:00; it now opens at 00:00 there (17:00 UTC the day before), and 4H bars run 08:00–12:00 and 12:00–16:00. Where a session crosses UTC midnight (Sydney, New York's evening hours in winter), the days' open, high, low, close and volume change too. A day the clocks change is 23 or 25 hours long. 1h and shorter bars keep their floor on the epoch, so the hour a fall-back night repeats stays two bars. Nothing changes without a zone, or with UTC: Binance, Bybit, Coinbase and Kraken build exactly what they built before.

Daily bars stamped at 00:00 UTC (as feeds and CSV files usually stamp them) are read as the dates they name: weeks and months built from them keep the Monday in its own week and their 00:00 UTC stamps, in any zone.

For a feed with `supportedTimeframes`, the wrapper that builds its missing timeframes asks `resolveSymbol` for the zone once per symbol, before the first history of such a timeframe (the chart's own question about the symbol is the same request), and keeps the zone, so history, older pages and live bars agree. It waits at most 5 s; 1h and shorter timeframes never wait. A feed without `resolveSymbol`, one that names no zone, fails to answer or answers after 5 s, or one whose timeframes can't line up with that midnight (only 4h bars for a zone 7 hours from UTC) builds UTC days as before, and keeps the timeframe; once a symbol's days are built in UTC they stay so, so older pages and the history after a reconnect never hold a day twice. After a failed or late answer they stay so only until the chart loads the symbol afresh (connects to it, or switches to it from another symbol or timeframe): that load builds them in the zone the feed has named since, or asks it again, without waiting a second time for an answer still on its way (`ResamplingAdapter.renewZone(symbol)`, which `StreamManager` calls on such a load). When a chart comes back to a symbol before the history of the one it left lands, and that load builds the symbol's days in a zone the feed has named since, the new history replaces the bars on screen instead of being merged with them; with the days in the same zone it is merged as before when older bars were paged in, keeping them and the view. `setMarket`'s zone doesn't move a feed's built bars. A zone the feed sends later in a `symbolInfo` event changes the times shown, not where built days start. The widget builds the days, weeks, months and 2h to 12h bars it made from bars it was given again when the symbol's zone or the market's arrives after them, the view staying on the times it showed; the shorter ones it made (15m or 1h from minutes) start where they did in any zone and stay as they are, the indicators computed once.

Daily bars show the date they stand for in any zone shown, on the time axis, the crosshair's label and its card, the tooltip Alt-click pins (which now writes a daily bar's date with its year, `10/23/2026`, where it wrote `10/22 17:00`) and the bar times a screen reader reads (the date alone): a bar stamped at 00:00 UTC its UTC date (a New York viewer saw the day before), a bar stamped at the exchange's midnight the exchange's date, any other bar the date in the zone shown (a bar stamped at the browser's midnight keeps its date).

New: `timeframeBucketStart(time, timeframe, { timeZone, weekStartsOn, dates })` and its `BucketOptions` type are exported from `@tradecanvas/chart`, to build your own bars where the chart's start (the third argument may still be the day weeks start on). `resampleBars` takes the same options, and `pickBaseTimeframe` a `{ timeZone }`. `chart.setMarket` sends a `marketChange` event. `TickAggregator` starts weeks on Monday and months on the 1st (it counted 7 and 30 days from 1970) and takes `setTimeZone(zone, dateStamps?)`; `StreamManager.setTimeZone(zone)` passes the chart's zone to it for `aggregateTicks` (and throws a RangeError for a zone the browser doesn't know, as `chart.setTimeZone` does, keeping the zone it has), and the bars it builds from ticks land on the history's: days are stamped at 00:00 UTC when the history's are, and 2h to 12h bars start at the exchange's midnight only when the history's do, so a feed's own 4h bars (00:00, 04:00… UTC) keep their times. On a timeframe built from a shorter one of the feed's they start where the built bars start. Built from hours, they start at the midnight of the zone the feed named for the symbol, else UTC's, whatever zone the chart has: a trade on a Friday evening in New York no longer lands in a day older than the history's last when the feed named no zone. Built from the feed's days stamped at 00:00 UTC, weeks and months keep the feed's dates, and a trade is dated in the zone the feed named, else the chart's, as on the feed's own days: a Monday morning trade in Sydney opens the new week. `ResamplingAdapter.bucketFor(symbol, timeframe)` says how a timeframe it builds is bucketed.

`@tradecanvas/commons` also exports the helpers these use: `timeframeFollowsDays(tf)` (whether a frame's buckets start at a midnight: days, weeks, months, 2h to 12h), `timeframeSpansDays(tf)` (bars a day long or more), `isUtcTimeZone(tz)`, and `barDateZone(time, tz, exchangeZone)` / `barDateParts(time, tz, exchangeZone)`, the zone and the calendar parts a daily bar is dated in.
