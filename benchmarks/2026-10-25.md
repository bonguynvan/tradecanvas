# Measurements, 2026-10-25

What a page downloads and how fast the chart draws, on `main` at `7ed02aa`
(1.48.0): the numbers the next changes to the engine's storage and feeds are
measured against. Each change of that kind puts its own before and after in
its pull request, from the same commands.

## What a page downloads

`pnpm build && pnpm size`: each entry bundled from the built packages as an
app's bundler would (Vite, minified, tree-shaken, split), in kB of 1,000
bytes. "First" is the entry and what it imports statically, what runs at
the start; "later" is what it loads with `import()`, here the WebGL
renderer when the chart's `renderer` is `'webgl'` or `'auto'`. Each chunk
is compressed on its own, as a server sends files: gzip at level 9, Brotli
at Node's default quality.

| Entry | First | gzip | br | Later | gzip | br |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `new Chart()` | 629.2 kB | 171.1 kB | 144.1 kB | 46.4 kB | 15.7 kB | 14.1 kB |
| `new ChartWidget()` | 1357.1 kB | 347.1 kB | 281.6 kB | 46.4 kB | 15.8 kB | 14.1 kB |
| the widget and one more language | 1396.4 kB | 358.8 kB | 289.5 kB | 46.4 kB | 15.8 kB | 14.1 kB |
| the widget and every language | 2408.0 kB | 642.5 kB | 453.6 kB | 46.4 kB | 15.8 kB | 14.1 kB |
| the whole package | 2541.7 kB | 682.3 kB | 486.1 kB | 46.4 kB | 15.7 kB | 14.1 kB |

- The headless chart is 171 kB in gzip; the widget doubles it. Nearly all
  of it loads at the start: the WebGL renderer, loaded later, is 16 kB.
- A language costs about 12 kB, and `registerWidgetLocales()` (all 30)
  nearly doubles the widget again: an app that needs a language or two
  should import just those.

## How fast it draws

`pnpm bench:render --dpr=1,2` and `--renderer=webgl`: headless Chrome
155 on an Intel UHD 770 (integrated, Core i7-14700, 32 GB), Windows 11,
nothing else running. A scene is panned for 150 frames.

A cell: the time to draw the whole scene, ms · the mean frame and its p95,
ms (16.7 is 60 fps) · frames over 20 ms, of 150.

### What it shows

- **At DPR 1 both renderers hold 60 fps** in all but the million bars
  zoomed out with indicators (S13) and, for Canvas 2D, 200,000 bars zoomed
  out with indicators (S6) and the 2560×1400 chart with indicators (S11:
  43 ms a scene, against 13 ms in WebGL).
- **At DPR 2 Canvas 2D drops frames in every scene** (31 to 149 of 150),
  with a scene at 6 to 89 ms;
  WebGL keeps 60 fps in 7 scenes of 14. On this GPU, compositing the
  full-size layers is a large part of a frame at DPR 2 (see the
  [performance page](https://tradecanvas.dev/docs/performance/)).
- **The phone holds 60 fps** in all four scenes; drawing 300 bars with
  four indicators takes 31 ms, the slowest.
